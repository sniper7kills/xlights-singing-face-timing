Feature: Transcription

Epic 2: Transcription   built

Purpose

The app runs a speech model in the browser and finds each sung word with its start time and its end time. The app groups the words into phrases. If the person has the lyrics, the app uses them to correct the words.

The person can select a remote endpoint in place of the speech model in the browser. A remote endpoint is a server that is compatible with the OpenAI transcription API, for example a self-hosted server of the person. The audio then leaves the browser, and the app shows the host before each run.

User stories

  • As the person, I want the app to find the words and their times, so that I do not

place each word by ear.

  • As the person, I want to paste the correct lyrics, so that wrong words from the

speech model do not reach my show.

  • As the person, I want to see progress, so that I know the app is working.
  • As the person, I want to transcribe one section again, so that I can fix a bad

part without losing the good parts.

  • As the person, I want to send the audio to my own Whisper server, so that a run

is fast on a computer with no fast graphics card.

Transcribe with vocal separation

  1. The person selects "Separate the vocals first" in the transcribe dialog.
  2. The person selects "Start".
  3. The app decodes the audio of the song or the range as two channels at 44.1 kHz.
  4. The separate worker downloads the vocal separation model if the browser does not

have it.

  1. The separate worker removes the instruments and returns the vocals as one

channel.

  1. The app converts the vocals to 16 kHz.
  2. The speech model or the remote endpoint reads the vocals, as for a run with no

separation.

  1. If the vocal separation fails, the dialog shows the message with "Continue with

no separation" and "Cancel". "Continue with no separation" goes on with the audio of the song.

  1. If Demucs fails for memory, the dialog also shows "Use the fast model". "Use the

fast model" runs the vocal separation again with Spleeter.

  1. With "Keep the separated tracks" on, the separate worker also returns the

instruments. At the end of the run, the app stores the vocals and the instruments at 22 050 Hz with the run report.

Put each singer on its own voice

  1. The person selects "Separate the vocals first".
  2. The person selects "Put each singer on its own voice (experimental)".
  3. The person selects "Start".
  4. The run separates the vocals, finds the words and writes the marks, as for a run

with vocal separation.

  1. The singers worker downloads the singer model if the browser does not have it.
  2. The singers worker finds the time ranges of each singer in the separated vocals.
  3. The app divides each phrase in which the singer changes, gives each phrase of the

run to its singers, and puts each singer on its own voice. See the singer rules in 05-voices-sections.md. With a count in "Singers", the worker finds that count of singers. With "Automatic", the worker estimates the count.

  1. The app closes the dialog. The toast names the count of voices, for example "214

words on 3 voices, 9 estimated".

Create phrases from lyrics without a speech model

  1. The person selects "Lyrics only" in the transcribe dialog.
  2. The person pastes the lyrics.
  3. If the lyrics have more words than the range has room for, the app asks before

the run. See the room rule in Behaviour rules.

  1. The app makes one phrase for each line.
  2. The app spreads the phrases evenly across the target range.
  3. The app spreads the words of each phrase evenly across the phrase.
  4. The app marks every word as "estimated".

Screens & states

Transcribe dialog. Regions: "Where to transcribe" choice, speech model selector or endpoint panel, device note, lyrics field, range selector (full song or the time selection), progress area, buttons.

The dialog is a column of three parts. The title stays at the top. The options scroll in the middle. The bottom part holds the remote warning, the progress area, the messages, each question and the buttons, and it does not scroll. So "Start" is fully visible at 1440x900 and at 1024x768 with each option on.

  • idle: selectors enabled, "Start" enabled.
  • preparing: "Preparing audio, 0:02 elapsed" with a bar that has no end. The app

decodes the audio and converts it to 16 kHz mono. "Cancel" enabled.

  • loading: "Loading speech model, 0:05 elapsed" with a bar that has no end. The

worker loads a speech model from the browser cache. "Cancel" enabled.

  • downloading: "Downloading speech model, 42%" with a bar. "Cancel" enabled.
  • started: "Transcribing, 0:12 elapsed" with a bar that has no end, until the first

progress of the run. "Cancel" enabled.

  • transcribing: "Transcribing, 1:12 of 3:40" with a bar. "Cancel" enabled. The

audio time changes at the end of each sung segment, a few seconds apart, and not only at the end of each 30-second chunk.

  • finishing: "Aligning lyrics and breaking down words".
  • confirm: "The run replaces the <n> marks of "<voice name>" from <start> to

<end>." with "Continue" and "Cancel". The count is the count of marks of the voice on all layers that overlap the range. The same rule decides that the dialog asks. The times have the form 0:01.500. For one mark, the text reads "the 1 mark".

  • confirm with the singer voices of the last run (FR-transcribe-149): "The run

replaces <n> marks from <start> to <end>: the marks of "<voice name>", and the marks of "Singer 2" and "Singer 3" from the last run." <n> is the sum of the marks of all named voices. The text names only a singer voice with a mark that overlaps the range. Three names or more read "Singer 2", "Singer 3" and "Singer 4". For one mark, the text reads "1 mark".

  • confirm for a run with no singer step on a song with singer voices of the last

run (FR-transcribe-150): the text adds "The voices of the last singer step do not change."

  • question: while the dialog asks, the progress area shows no text and no bar,

because the run waits for the answer.

  • stopped: after a cancel, the dialog goes back to idle and shows "The run stopped.

The marks did not change." for 5 seconds.

  • Escape during a run moves the focus to "Cancel" and shows "Select Cancel to stop

the run." The run goes on. Escape at a question answers "Cancel".

  • error: the message, with "Retry" and "Close".
  • done: the dialog closes. A toast shows "214 words, 9 estimated".

The device note reads "Uses your graphics card (WebGPU)" or "WebGPU is not available. Transcription is slower on this browser."

  • The elapsed time counts from the "Start" click and changes each second. The

Behaviour rules

  • The default speech model is base. The app remembers the last selection.
  • Without lyrics, a new phrase starts when one of these is true:
    • the gap from the end of the previous word is 700 ms or more,
    • the previous word ends with ., ? or !,
    • the phrase has 12 words.
  • A phrase starts at the start of its first word and ends at the end of its last

word.

  • The app removes these characters from word labels: . , ! ? ; : " ( ) [ ]. The

app keeps apostrophes and hyphens inside a word.

  • Word labels keep the letter case from the lyrics, or from the speech model when

there are no lyrics.

  • The app discards a word that the speech model returns with no end time.
  • A word is at least one frame size long. The app extends a shorter word to one

frame size, and moves the next word if needed.

  • Words on one layer do not overlap. If the speech model returns an overlap, the

earlier word ends where the later word starts.

  • Characters of the lyrics. A lyrics word is a part of a line between spaces.
    • The app uses a word that has a letter or a digit, in any script. For example

"café", "99" and "你好" are words.

  • The app leaves out a part with no letter and no digit, for example "&", "—" or

an emoji, and counts it as a word that it cannot use.

  • The label rules above then apply to each word.
    • The dictionaries have English words only. A word that no dictionary has is an

unknown word, for example a word in another script.

  • Room rule. The room of a range is one word for each frame size of the range.

The app keeps a word one frame size long or more, so more words cannot fit.

  • Before a run with lyrics, the app counts the words of each lyrics line.
    • The app keeps the lines from line 1 while their words fit in the room. It

leaves out each line after the first line that does not fit.

  • The app always keeps line 1. If line 1 alone does not fit, the app keeps its

earliest words that fit.

  • At 50 ms, a song of 28.9 s has room for 578 words. 200 lines of 7 words keep

lines 1 to 82.

  • More than about 4 words for each second gives words of one or two frames. The

face then changes too fast to read. The room rule does not warn about this.

  • A word of the speech model that has no letter and no digit after the label rules

gives no mark. The app counts it as a word that it cannot use.

  • Align rules. The app compares the transcribed words with the lyrics words as

two sequences, without case and without punctuation.

  • A lyrics word that matches a transcribed word takes its time.
    • A lyrics word between two matched words with no match gets an even share of the

time between them, and is "estimated".

  • A transcribed word with no lyrics match is discarded.
    • If fewer than 30% of the lyrics words match, the app shows a warning before it

creates marks.

  • A range run replaces the marks that overlap the range:
    • A word that overlaps the range is removed with its phonemes.
    • A phrase that crosses an edge of the range gets shorter and keeps its words

that are outside the range.

  • A phrase that contains the range becomes two phrases, one on each side.
    • A phrase with no word left is removed.
    • A phrase that changes takes the text of its kept words as its label.
    • Marks outside the range do not change. The replace is one undo step.
  • Singer voices of the last run (issue #174):
    • The singer voices of the last run are the voices that the singer step of the

last run added and that the song still has. The app finds them by the voice ids of the run data, so a renamed voice is still such a voice. Run data from before issue #173 has no ids, so the app finds them by the names in the report.

  • The voice of the new run is never a singer voice of the last run, also when

the last run added it.

  • A speech model run with "Put each singer on its own voice" replaces the marks

in the range of each singer voice of the last run, by the range rules above. Marks that the person added by hand inside the range go too.

  • A singer voice of the last run with no mark left after the replace goes. So

the singer step of the run can use the name "Singer 2" again. A singer voice with marks outside the range stays, and the new singer voices get the next free names.

  • The replace of all voices and the removed voices are one undo step with the

marks of the run. The undo steps of the run in order are: the singer step, the sections of the tags, then the marks with the replace. Each undo restores the song exactly, with the ids, the order, the colours and the mute state of each voice.

  • A run with no singer step leaves the singer voices of the last run as they

are. A lyrics-only run has no singer step.

  • The worker handles one run at a time. The "Transcribe" action is disabled during

a run.

  • The app transcribes in 30-second chunks with a 5-second stride.
  • The language is English. The task is transcribe.
  • Remote endpoint rules.
    • The app removes the spaces and the trailing slashes of the base URL. It keeps

the path as typed and adds /audio/transcriptions.

  • The request is multipart/form-data with the fields file (audio.wav,

audio/wav), model, language=en, response_format=verbose_json and timestamp_granularities[]=word.

  • The file is 16-bit PCM WAV with one channel at 16 kHz, about 1.9 MB for each

minute.

  • If the file is larger than 25 000 000 bytes, the app sends nothing and shows

the message for a file above the limit. The limit is about 13 minutes of audio.

  • If the API key is empty, the request has no Authorization header. A

self-hosted server can work with no key.

  • The app adds the start of the range to each word time. The app discards a word

with no start time, no end time or no text.

  • A lyrics-only run never sends a request, whatever the choice.
    • An error message never holds the API key or the text of the response.

Data touched

  • Writes RUN_DATA for the song at the end of each completed run: the voices of

the run, the audio tracks, the singer ranges and the run report. A new run replaces the full record.

  • Reads RUN_DATA before the replace question, for the singer voices of the last

run.

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.keepTracks, the last choice of "Keep the separated

tracks". A record with no value gives true.

  • Reads AUDIO_FILE. Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.speechModelId.
  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.transcribeSource and

APP_SETTINGS.remoteEndpoint (baseUrl, apiKey, model). The API key stays in IndexedDB and goes only to the base URL.

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.separateVocals, the last choice of "Separate the

vocals first".

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.separationModel ('spleeter' | 'demucs'), the

last choice of "Separation model". A value that the app does not know gives 'spleeter'.

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.singerVoices, the last choice of "Put each singer

on its own voice".

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.singerCount ('automatic' | 2 | 3 | 4), the last

choice of "Singers". A value that the app does not know gives 'automatic'.

  • Reads and writes APP_SETTINGS.deviceSpeeds: for each speech model, the measured

speed of each device and the time of the last webgpu failure. The app selects the device from these values.

  • Writes MARK on the phrase, word and phoneme layers of the selected voice.
  • Adds the field estimated: bool to MARK. The field is false after the person

moves the mark.

  • The speech model files are in the browser Cache Storage, managed by the library.

Interfaces

@huggingface/transformers 4.3.2. Verified against: https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/api/pipelines, https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/en/guides/webgpu, https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/en/guides/dtypes (retrieved 2026-11-24).

  • Create: pipeline('automatic-speech-recognition', modelId, { device, dtype }).
  • device: 'webgpu' selects WebGPU. The default in a browser is WASM.
  • dtype values include fp32, fp16, q8, q4. The docs state that Whisper is

sensitive to the quantisation of the encoder.

  • Call: `transcriber(audio, { return_timestamps: 'word', chunk_length_s: 30,

stride_length_s: 5, language, task })`.

  • Audio input types: string | URL | Float32Array | Float64Array.
  • Result: { text, chunks: [{ text, timestamp: [start, end] }] }, times in seconds.

Confirmed on 2026-11-24 for version 4.3.2. Pull request #71 cites each source.

  • Model repositories. The app uses onnx-community/whisper-tiny_timestamped,

onnx-community/whisper-base_timestamped and onnx-community/whisper-small_timestamped. Each repository has alignment_heads in generation_config.json, and the library needs them for return_timestamps: 'word'. Source: https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/whisper-base_timestamped/resolve/main/generation_config.json (the same path for tiny and small).

  • dtype. { encoder_model: 'fp32', decoder_model_merged: 'q8' }. The app also

sets the key model: 'fp32', because the cache check of the library reads that key. Source: https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/en/guides/dtypes

  • Download sizes. tiny 66 MB, base 139 MB, small 512 MB, for that dtype

(1 MB = 1 000 000 bytes). Source: https://huggingface.co/api/models/onnx-community/whisper-*_timestamped/tree/main/onnx

  • Download progress. The pipeline option is progress_callback. The event

{ status: 'progress_total', name, progress, loaded, total, files } gives the bytes of all files. Source: https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/api/pipelines and node_modules/@huggingface/transformers/types/utils/core.d.ts (ProgressInfo).

  • Input sample rate. A Float32Array input must be at 16 kHz. The

preprocessor_config.json of each repository sets "sampling_rate": 16000, and the library does not resample a Float32Array. Source: https://huggingface.co/onnx-community/whisper-base_timestamped/resolve/main/preprocessor_config.json

  • Stop a run. The library has no stop operation for a pipeline call. The client

terminates the worker. Source: node_modules/@huggingface/transformers/types/pipelines/automatic-speech-recognition.d.ts

  • Progress inside a chunk. The pipeline call accepts the generate option

streamer. The typed pipeline options accept a TextStreamer. generate calls streamer.put(tokens) for the prompt and for each new token, and streamer.end() at the end of each generate pass. With word times, Whisper writes timestamp tokens. A timestamp token id minus timestamp_begin of the tokenizer, times 0.02 s, gives a time inside the 30-second window of the pass. Whisper can read one chunk in more than one pass, and the next pass starts at the end of the last complete segment. The worker extends TextStreamer with its own put and end, so it decodes no text. Sources: https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers.js/api/generation/streamers and, in node_modules/@huggingface/transformers/src/, generation/streamers.js, models/whisper/modeling_whisper.js (_generate_with_seek) and pipelines/automatic-speech-recognition.js (_call_whisper).

  • Audio decode. The app decodes each audio file in an OfflineAudioContext

with one channel, a length of one frame, and the sample rate of the result. An OfflineAudioContext plays no sound, so the autoplay policy of the browser does not apply to it. decodeAudioData resamples the audio to the sample rate of the context. Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OfflineAudioContext/OfflineAudioContext, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BaseAudioContext/decodeAudioData, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay and https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/ (retrieved 2026-11-24).

OpenAI transcription API. Verified against: https://developers.openai.com/api/reference/resources/audio/subresources/transcriptions/methods/create and https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/speech-to-text (retrieved 2026-11-24).

  • Operation: POST /audio/transcriptions with multipart/form-data.
  • Fields: file and model are required. language (ISO-639-1), prompt,

response_format (default json) and timestamp_granularities[] (default segment) are optional.

  • timestamp_granularities[] needs response_format=verbose_json. The guide states

that it "is only supported for whisper-1". gpt-4o-transcribe and gpt-4o-mini-transcribe support json only, so they return no word times.

  • verbose_json has words, a list of { word, start, end } with times in

seconds.

  • Formats: flac, mp3, mp4, mpeg, mpga, m4a, ogg, wav, webm. "Files can be up to

25 MB."

Self-hosted server: speaches (faster-whisper-server). Verified against: https://speaches.ai/configuration/, https://speaches.ai/usage/speech-to-text/ and the source files src/speaches/routers/stt.py, src/speaches/executors/whisper.py and src/speaches/main.py of speaches-ai/speaches on the master branch (retrieved 2026-11-24).

response_format=verbose_json. "word" in the list turns on word times, and the response has words with word, start and end.

  • CORS is off by default. The variable ALLOW_ORIGINS (for example

'["https://<app domain>"]') turns it on for all methods and headers.

  • The setting api_key is unset by default. When it is set, each request needs the

key.

Browser rules. Verified against MDN (retrieved 2026-11-24): https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Mixed_content, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Guides/CORS/Errors, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/XMLHttpRequest/upload, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/fetch and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Reference/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/connect-src.   • A page from an https address cannot call an http address. A loopback address

such as http://localhost or http://127.0.0.1 is not mixed content.

sees the same error as for no connection.

exist before send(), and they make a cross-origin request send a CORS preflight. fetch gives no upload progress, so the app uses XMLHttpRequest.

asks the person for the permission first.

{ type: 'error'; runId?: string; kind: ErrorKind; detail: string }

TimedWord is { text: string; startMs: number; endMs: number }. The client stops a run by terminating the worker, and the next run loads the speech model again from the browser cache.

Vocal separation, src/workers/separate.worker.ts. The types are in src/features/vocal-separation/protocol.ts. The client separateVocals(audio, { onDownload, onProgress, signal, model }) starts one worker for each run and terminates it at the end or at "Cancel". The worker makes the engine of model. A request with no model uses 'spleeter'. The Demucs engine and demucs-web are a separate chunk that the worker imports only for 'demucs'.

interface StereoAudio { left: Float32Array; right: Float32Array; sampleRate: 44100 }
// to worker (SeparateRequest)
{ type: 'separate'; runId: number; audio: StereoAudio; model?: 'spleeter' | 'demucs'; instruments?: boolean }
// from worker (SeparateMessage)
{ type: 'download'; runId: number; loadedBytes: number; totalBytes: number }
{ type: 'progress'; runId: number; doneMs: number; totalMs: number }
{ type: 'result'; runId: number; vocals: Float32Array; instruments?: Float32Array } // one channel each, 44.1 kHz, the length of the input

The worker computes and sends instruments only for a request with instruments: true. Spleeter gives the input with the accompaniment mask, 1 minus the vocals mask. This costs one more inverse STFT and no model run. Demucs gives the sum of the drums, bass and other stems.

{ type: 'error'; runId: number; kind: 'download' | 'memory' | 'model' | 'unknown'; detail: string }

The app converts the vocals to 16 kHz in an OfflineAudioContext at 16 000 Hz: a buffer source plays a buffer at 44 100 Hz, and startRendering() returns the resampled AudioBuffer. A buffer can have a sample rate that is not the rate of the context, and the browser resamples it. Sources: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/OfflineAudioContext/startRendering, https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BaseAudioContext/createBuffer and https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioBuffer/copyToChannel (retrieved 2026-11-24).

Singer step, src/workers/singers.worker.ts. The types are in src/features/singer-voices/protocol.ts. The client findSingers(vocals, { singerCount, onDownload, onProgress, signal }) starts one worker for each run and terminates it at the end or at "Cancel". The worker calls the engine of createSingerFinder() in src/features/singer-voices/engine/index.ts. When __SINGERS_FIXTURE__ is true, the client starts singersFixture.worker.ts, which downloads no model.

interface SingerSegment { startMs: number; endMs: number; singer: number } // singer 0, 1, 2 … in order of first appearance
// to worker (SingersRequest)

// from worker (SingersMessage)
{ type: 'download'; runId: number; loadedBytes: number; totalBytes: number }
{ type: 'progress'; runId: number; doneMs: number; totalMs: number }

{ type: 'error'; runId: number; kind: 'download' | 'memory' | 'model' | 'unknown'; detail: string }

The times of a segment start at the start of the vocals. A range run adds the start of the range.

The engine uses two ONNX models from the Hugging Face Hub, through @huggingface/transformers: onnx-community/pyannote-segmentation-3.0 (MIT) and onnx-community/wespeaker-voxceleb-resnet34-LM (CC BY 4.0), 32 522 457 bytes together. The accuracy on one duet and two test mixes is in the ADR. See docs/adr/0009-singer-voices-from-diarization.md and docs/licences/singer-models.md.

Singer diagnostics

The singer search counts these numbers with no extra model call:

  • The audio time, the count of windows, the frames with a singer and the sung time.
  • The count of embeddings, and the embeddings with a value that is not finite.
  • The settings in force: the threshold between the centroids of two singers, the

minimum group size, the highest count of singers, and the two limits of the rule for short singers.

  • The raw groups: the groups of the merge round that the clustering selects, before

each later join. See docs/adr/0009-singer-voices-from-diarization.md.

  • For each raw group: its sung time, its count of embeddings, and its result. A raw

group stays as a singer, or joins another raw group for one of these reasons:

  • "fewer than <n> embeddings"
    • "over the highest count"
    • "under <p>% of the sung time"
    • "under <s> seconds"
  • The smallest, the median and the largest distance between the centroids of the

raw groups, so that a reader can compare them with the threshold.

  • The final singers: for each, its sung time and its count of ranges.

The text writes the raw groups and the final singers as tables with aligned columns.

Out of scope

  • Separation of stems other than the vocals.
  • Vocal separation on a server.
  • Detecting which singer sings each word. The singer step gives singers to phrases.
  • Two clean audio signals for singers who sing at the same time.
  • The names of the singers.
  • Languages other than English.
  • Speech models larger than small.
  • A server or a proxy that belongs to the app.
  • A remote endpoint that is not compatible with the OpenAI transcription API.
  • A streaming response from a remote endpoint.
